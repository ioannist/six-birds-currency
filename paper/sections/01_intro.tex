\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Currencies keep reappearing}

Energy in physics, code length in information theory, money in economics, compute in algorithms, and attention in cognition are introduced in different languages, yet they do the same mathematical work. They limit which states or protocols are feasible. They add up along sequences of operations. And they come back as exchange rates between goals that cannot all be met at once: inverse temperature trades entropy against energy, a market price trades one good against another, a regularization strength trades fit against complexity \cite{jaynes1957information,shannon1948mathematical,arrowdebreu1954,sims2003rationalinattention}. We call such quantities \emph{currencies}.

Standard duality theory explains part of this \cite{boyd2004convex}. Once an optimization problem is written down, with its variables and its constraints, Lagrange multipliers are exactly what one should expect. But that explanation starts late. It takes the descriptive layer and its constraints as given. It does not say where a constraint at one level of description comes from, or why it so often looks like a limit on something spent at a lower level.

This paper supplies that missing step, and is careful about what the step requires. The idea is simple. A higher-level object, such as a macrostate, a packaged pattern, or a stable representation, persists only if something at the lower level keeps maintaining it. When that maintenance draws on a limited resource, the resource bound becomes a feasibility constraint at the higher level. If the higher layer then settles into its least-committed (maximum-entropy) description subject to that constraint, a multiplier appears, and the multiplier is the higher layer's own currency: the exchange rate between what it would like to do and what the lower layer can afford. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows the chain.

The qualifier matters. Persistence by itself does not imply a budget: a system that stays in one state forever persists perfectly and spends nothing. The principle therefore takes the resource law as a premise and proves what follows from it. Within that scope the conclusions are theorems, not analogies.

\subsection{The Six Birds setting}

Six Birds is a closure calculus for emergent description \cite{tsiokos2026sixbirds}. It is built from six primitives: \textbf{P1} operator rewrite, \textbf{P2} constraints or gating, \textbf{P3} protocol holonomy (route dependence), \textbf{P4} staging or sectors, \textbf{P5} packaging, and \textbf{P6} accounting or audit. This paper is about the chain \textbf{P5}\(\to\)\textbf{P6}\(\to\)\textbf{P2}: packaging creates higher-level objects, accounting measures what it costs to keep them coherent, and constraints turn that cost into the boundary of what the next layer can do.

The framework also tells us what does \emph{not} count. A quantity is a currency only if it does structural work: it gates feasibility, adds along protocols, drives a monotone potential, or prices a constraint. A statistic that merely correlates with behaviour is not enough, and neither is bare route dependence. Protocol mismatch becomes a directionality currency only when an honest audit backs it up, and an audit is honest only if coarse observation cannot manufacture it.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{A layer-relative definition of currency} (Section~\ref{sec:framework}) with four roles (constraint, ledger, potential, dual price) and the nontriviality conditions that separate them from arbitrary statistics.
    \item \textbf{A conditional currency--constraint principle} (Section~\ref{sec:duality}). Given a maintenance ledger and a resource law, the ledger induces a higher-layer feasibility constraint. In a finite entropy closure the Gibbs law is optimal and its multiplier is a supporting shadow price (Theorem~\ref{thm:duality}). We describe the full price curve, including infeasible, infinite-price, active, and slack regimes (Proposition~\ref{prop:regimes}).
    \item \textbf{Honest audits} (Section~\ref{sec:audits}). Path-reversal relative entropy cannot increase under any deterministic pointwise observation. A simple regularization keeps empirical audits finite while preserving that inequality on the same sample. Cycle affinities give low-dimensional drive coordinates.
    \item \textbf{A finite Markov laboratory} (Section~\ref{sec:lab}) that illustrates each piece and separates what is forced by theorem from what depends on the chosen example. This includes a uniform bound showing that stronger maintenance pricing makes packaged objects nearly idempotent (Theorem~\ref{thm:packaging}).
    \item \textbf{A Lean~4 formalization} (Appendix~\ref{app:lean}) of finite supported KL data processing, its path-reversal specialization, and single-row Gibbs duality, using only the standard axioms.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Place in the Six Birds series}

\emph{Six Birds: Foundations of Emergence Calculus} introduced the primitives and the layer package of lenses, packaging, and audits \cite{tsiokos2026sixbirds}. \emph{To Create a Stone with Six Birds} turned them into an interaction algebra (PICA) and a finite stochastic laboratory \cite{tsiokos2026create}. The present paper adds the economics of closure: what a closure must spend to keep its objects, how that spending becomes the next layer's constraint, and when the next layer answers with a price.
