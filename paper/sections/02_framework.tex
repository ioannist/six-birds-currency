\section{Currencies relative to a layer}\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Layers as closure packages}

In Six Birds a descriptive layer is a closure package, not a metaphysical level \cite{tsiokos2026sixbirds}. It consists of a finite substrate, a lens that fixes what can be said at that resolution, a packaging rule that turns recurrent structure into objects, and an audit that certifies what survives coarse observation. In the notation of the foundations paper,
\[
\mathcal T=(Z,f,\Sigma_f,E,\mathcal A),
\]
where \(Z\) is a finite substrate, \(f\) a lens, \(\Sigma_f\) the induced definability algebra, \(E\) a packaging endomap, and \(\mathcal A\) an audit functional.

Currencies are always relative to such a package. The same number can play different roles at different resolutions. Energy is a conserved or budgeted quantity for microscopic dynamics; at the ensemble level the natural coordinate is inverse temperature, the multiplier of the mean-energy constraint. A regularization strength is not a currency of the task being learned; it is a currency of the learning layer, which uses it to decide which updates it can afford.

\subsection{Four structural roles}

Fix a layer with state space \(Y\). A currency is a low-dimensional map \(u:Y\to\R^k\), usually with \(k\ll|Y|\), that plays at least one of the following roles.

\paragraph{Constraint currency (C1).} Feasibility is expressed through \(u\): the feasible set is
\[
K=\{y\in Y:\ g(u(y))\le 0\}
\]
or an intersection of such sets. This is the primal view. The currency is what the layer must keep within bounds.

\paragraph{Ledger currency (C2).} Costs add along protocols. For a protocol \(\gamma=(y_0\to y_1\to\cdots\to y_T)\),
\[
\mathrm{Cost}(\gamma)=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\ell\big(u(y_t),\,y_t\to y_{t+1}\big),
\]
exactly or in expectation. This is the accounting view.

\paragraph{Potential currency (C3).} A scalar potential \(U\), built from the ledger, decreases along trajectories: \(U(y_{t+1})\le U(y_t)\) up to controlled noise, or \(\dot U\le 0\) in continuous time. This is the Lyapunov or thermodynamic view.

\paragraph{Dual currency (C4).} The layer closes by optimizing under constraints, and the currency is a shadow price. For a concave objective \(\Phi\) and an inequality constraint,
\[
V(b)=\sup_{q\in\mathcal Q}\big\{\Phi(q):\ \E_q[f]\le b\big\},
\]
a multiplier \(\lambda\ge0\) prices the constraint. It satisfies \(V(b')\le V(b)+\lambda(b'-b)\) for all \(b'\); that is, \(\lambda\) is a supergradient of the concave value function. Where \(V\) is differentiable this reads \(\lambda=V'(b)\). An inequality constraint gives a nonnegative price, while an equality constraint gives a multiplier of either sign. A constraint can also be active with price zero; in the finite entropy closure of Section~\ref{sec:duality} this happens exactly at the threshold \(b=c_0\) where the unconstrained optimum just meets the budget (Proposition~\ref{prop:regimes}).

These roles come from standard places. Shadow prices are the dual objects of constrained optimization \cite{boyd2004convex}; money has a well-known interpretation as a ledger or memory device \cite{kocherlakota1998money}; and thermodynamic potentials arise as constrained duals in the maximum-entropy programme \cite{jaynes1957information}.

\paragraph{Nontriviality.} Read literally, each role can be satisfied trivially. A constant \(u\) with \(K=Y\) meets C1; an edge cost that ignores \(u\) meets C2; a constant potential meets C3. To distinguish structural currencies from arbitrary statistics we therefore require essential use. The feasibility boundary must be proper (\(K\ne Y\)) and must depend on \(u\). The ledger increment must depend on \(u\). The potential must be nonconstant and tied to the ledger. And a dual currency must belong to a constraint that can bind. For the dual role the condition has a precise form. A price is identifiable only if the cost is nonconstant in some row that carries weight (Proposition~\ref{prop:regimes}); every cost used in Section~\ref{sec:lab} has this property.

\subsection{Structural currencies, proxies, and path quantities}

Many quantities called currencies in applied work are proxies. Trust scores, prestige metrics, and ad hoc complexity measures can correlate with feasibility or performance without entering the layer's constraints, ledgers, or dual programmes. They may be useful summaries, but they are not currencies in the sense used here. Section~\ref{sec:lab-proxy} makes the distinction concrete. A cost with exactly the same values in each row, but attached to the wrong transitions, predicts worse than the cost read off the lower-layer ledger.

Currencies can live on states or on paths. A state currency is a function \(u(y)\). A path currency is a functional of whole trajectories: an action, an accumulated entropy production, or a path-reversal asymmetry. Six Birds allows both, because audits can be defined on states, edges, or paths \cite{tsiokos2026sixbirds}. What matters is the structural role, not the representation.

\subsection{Why route dependence is not enough}

Protocol mismatch, or holonomy (\textbf{P3}), is often the first sign that a system remembers how it was driven. But route dependence alone is not a directionality currency. A protocol can fail to commute simply because a schedule variable has been hidden, while the enlarged autonomous system is perfectly reversible. What counts is an audit that detects irreversible drive and that coarse observation cannot inflate. Path-reversal relative entropy is the classical choice: irreversibility is measured by comparing the law of forward paths with the law of reversed paths \cite{kullback1951information,lebowitz1999gallavotti,seifert2005entropy}. Section~\ref{sec:audits} proves that this audit can be hidden by observation but never created by it, and builds the empirical version used in the laboratory.
